# SAMA Customer Psychology

## What the Customer Is Really Asking

Every time a SAMA customer looks at a garment, she is asking:

1. **"Will this make me look elegant?"** — Not just pretty. Elegant. Put-together. Like she made an effort without trying too hard.
2. **"Can I wear this to work?"** — Versatility matters. She doesn't want a wardrobe full of single-occasion pieces.
3. **"Can I wear it more than once?"** — She's practical. She wants value. She'll rewear something she loves.
4. **"Does it look expensive?"** — She wants to look like she spent more than she did. Perceived value is real value.
5. **"Will it suit my body?"** — She has concerns. She wants flattering, not just fashionable.
6. **"Is it comfortable?"** — She's not willing to suffer for fashion. Comfort is non-negotiable.
7. **"Can I style it differently?"** — She wants options. One garment, multiple looks.
8. **"Is it worth the price?"** — She'll pay for quality and design. She won't pay for a label.
9. **"Will I get compliments?"** — She wants to feel seen and admired.
10. **"Does it feel different from what everyone else is wearing?"** — She wants to stand out, but not in a loud way. Quietly distinctive.

## Design Implications
- Every garment must answer at least 5 of these questions with a "yes"
- Silhouettes must be flattering across body types — not just on a size 0
- Details must be interesting enough to generate compliments
- Fabric must feel as good as it looks
- Price must feel justified by the quality and design

## Body-Inclusive Thinking
- Design for real bodies, not just sample sizes
- Offer adjustable details: tie-waists, elasticated backs, adjustable straps
- Offer length options where possible
- Avoid designs that only work on one body type
- Never use forced or promotional body-positive messaging — just design inclusively and let the clothes speak
