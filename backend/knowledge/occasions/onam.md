# Onam Fact Sheet

## Cultural Context
Onam is Kerala's biggest harvest festival, celebrated over 10 days (Atham to Thiruvonam). It is a state-wide celebration cutting across religions. The central ritual is the Onam Sadhya (feast) and Pookalam (flower carpet). Families gather, visit relatives, and dress in traditional attire.

## Traditional Dress Norms
- Traditional: Kasavu (off-white Kerala cotton with gold zari border) for women — the set mundu or Kerala saree
- SAMA interpretation: off-white and gold as a colour story applied to kurti sets, co-ords, and dresses — NOT sarees or blouses
- Customers want to look festive and Kerala-rooted without wearing a full traditional set
- Gold accents, kasavu-inspired borders, and subtle zari details are appropriate

## Colours
- Primary: off-white (cream, ivory, natural white), gold, pale gold
- Secondary: soft green (like paddy fields), muted terracotta, dusty rose
- Avoid: bright neons, heavy black, stark white without warmth

## Fabrics
- Appropriate: cotton, linen, cotton-silk blends, soft georgette, chanderi
- Kerala climate is warm and humid in August/September — breathable fabrics are essential
- Avoid: heavy velvet, thick brocade, synthetic fabrics that trap heat

## What to Avoid
- Do not suggest sarees, blouses, or traditional set mundus — SAMA does not make these
- Do not suggest heavy embellishment — Onam aesthetic is clean and elegant, not heavily embroidered
- Do not invent facts about handloom unless confirmed by the owner
- Avoid Diwali or North Indian festival references (no diyas, rangoli, or Diwali-specific motifs)

## Timing
- Onam falls in August or September (Malayalam month of Chingam)
- Customers start ordering stitching 3–4 weeks before Thiruvonam
- Content should begin 3 weeks before the festival
- Ordering deadline content: 2 weeks before Thiruvonam
