# Diwali Fact Sheet

## Cultural Context
Diwali is the festival of lights, celebrated primarily by Hindu communities. In Kerala it is observed but is not as dominant as Onam. For SAMA's customer base (mixed, urban, South Indian), Diwali is a reason to dress up — parties, family visits, gifting. The aesthetic is rich, warm, and celebratory.

## Traditional Dress Norms
- Festive Indian wear — but SAMA's interpretation is contemporary, not traditional
- Customers want to look festive without wearing a full traditional outfit
- Indo-western silhouettes (kurti sets, co-ords, dresses with Indian details) work well

## Colours
- Primary: deep jewel tones — emerald, ruby red, royal blue, deep purple, burnt orange
- Secondary: gold accents, mustard, warm terracotta
- Metallics (gold, copper, bronze) as accents are very appropriate
- Avoid: pastels, off-white (too Onam-coded), stark white

## Fabrics
- October/November in South India is warm — breathable fabrics with a festive look
- Appropriate: georgette, crepe, chanderi, cotton-silk blends, soft brocade accents
- Avoid: heavy velvet, thick brocade as the main fabric — too hot and too heavy for the climate

## What to Avoid
- Do not reference Onam or Christmas
- Do not suggest sarees or blouses
- Do not use Diwali-specific North Indian references (diyas, rangoli) as design motifs unless the owner confirms
- Do not invent facts about handloom or sustainability

## Timing
- Diwali falls in October or November
- Customers start ordering 3–4 weeks before
- Content should begin 3 weeks before Diwali
- Ordering deadline: 2 weeks before Diwali

## Content Angles
- "Diwali dressing, the SAMA way" (contemporary Indian festive)
- Jewel tones and gold — colour story content
- Kids' Diwali outfit matching adult piece
- "From Diwali puja to the party" (versatility)
- Behind the scenes: gold accent detail being added
