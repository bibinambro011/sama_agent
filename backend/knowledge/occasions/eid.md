# Eid Fact Sheet

## Cultural Context
Eid al-Fitr (end of Ramadan) and Eid al-Adha are major celebrations for Muslim customers. In Kerala, Eid is a significant occasion with new clothes being an important tradition. Family visits, prayers, and feasts are central. The aesthetic is elegant, modest, and festive.

## Traditional Dress Norms
- Modesty is important: covered arms (at least elbow length), modest necklines, longer lengths
- Customers want to look elegant and festive while maintaining modesty
- Flowing silhouettes, longer kurtis, wide-leg pants, and modest co-ords work well
- SAMA's interpretation: modest but contemporary — not traditional ethnic wear

## Colours
- Primary: white, ivory, soft pastels (mint, blush, lavender), rich jewel tones
- Secondary: gold accents, silver, soft greens
- White and ivory are particularly auspicious for Eid
- Avoid: black as the primary colour (though accents are fine)

## Fabrics
- Eid in Kerala falls in warm weather (varies by year) — breathable fabrics essential
- Appropriate: georgette, chiffon, soft cotton, linen, crepe
- Flowing fabrics that drape well and look elegant
- Avoid: stiff or heavy fabrics

## What to Avoid
- Do not reference other festivals (Onam, Christmas, Diwali)
- Ensure all suggested garments are modest (sleeve length, neckline, hem length)
- Do not invent facts about handloom or certifications

## Timing
- Eid date varies by year (lunar calendar)
- Customers start ordering 3–4 weeks before Eid
- Content should begin 3 weeks before
- Ordering deadline: 2 weeks before Eid
