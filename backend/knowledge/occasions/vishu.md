# Vishu Fact Sheet

## Cultural Context
Vishu is the Kerala Hindu New Year, celebrated in April. The central ritual is the Vishukkani — an auspicious arrangement of items viewed first thing in the morning. Vishu Kaineettam (gift of money) is given to children and elders. It is a family celebration, slightly less commercially driven than Onam but still an occasion for new clothes.

## Traditional Dress Norms
- Similar to Onam: kasavu (off-white and gold) is traditional
- SAMA interpretation: off-white and gold palette applied to contemporary silhouettes
- Lighter, more casual than Onam — Vishu is a morning celebration, not a full-day formal event
- Kids' wear is particularly important — Vishu Kaineettam makes new clothes for children a strong tradition

## Colours
- Primary: off-white, gold, pale yellow (the colour of kani konna flowers — golden shower tree)
- Secondary: soft green, muted terracotta
- Yellow and gold are particularly auspicious for Vishu (kani konna flowers)
- Avoid: heavy dark colours, bright neons

## Fabrics
- April in Kerala is very hot — lightweight breathable fabrics are essential
- Appropriate: cotton, linen, cotton-linen blends, light georgette
- Avoid: any heavy or synthetic fabric

## What to Avoid
- Do not reference Christmas or Diwali
- Do not confuse with Onam — Vishu is April, Onam is August/September
- Vishu is more understated than Onam — avoid over-the-top festive suggestions
